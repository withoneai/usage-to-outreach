# Usage outreach policy

What to say once the code has already decided an account is worth writing to.
Whether an account is a spike or a crater is arithmetic (see README, "How the
call gets made"); this file is only the voice.

## Spikes: an account using the product a lot more than last week

- Open with the specific number, not a generic compliment. "You ran 410
  actions last week, up from 150" beats "we noticed you've been busy."
- Tie the number to one concrete next-tier benefit relevant to that kind of
  growth (more seats, higher limits, priority support), not a generic
  "let's chat about your plan."
- Offer a 15-minute call, but make the email useful on its own if they never
  book one.
- Tone: congratulatory, not salesy. This is good news for them before it is
  an opportunity for us.

## Craters: an account using the product a lot less than last week

- Lead with a plain check-in, not an alarm. Ask if something broke, if a
  workflow changed, or if the person who used it moved teams. Never say
  "we noticed you're about to churn" or anything that implies we are
  watching them leave.
- Offer something concrete: a 15-minute call, a point of contact, or an
  invite to flag what is not working.
- Tone: genuinely helpful. The honest goal of this email is to find out what
  happened, not to save the account by talking faster.

## Both

- Reference the account's own numbers and plan; never a competitor, a
  discount, or a contract term not given to you.
- Under 150 words. No subject line tricks ("quick question").
- Sign off: "— The One team, success@withone.ai"
- No em dashes or en dashes inside the body text itself (the sign-off above
  is the one fixed exception). Use a comma, period, colon, or parentheses.
